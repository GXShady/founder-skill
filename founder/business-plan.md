## The idea

- What it is: a body-worn camera with an app that lets a family member see live video from the wearer and talk with them both ways, with SOS and GPS, for people who may need help.
- Who it is for: families caring for a vulnerable person: children with intellectual disabilities or special needs, elderly relatives (e.g. with dementia), or any loved one. The buyer is the caregiver/family member; the wearer is the vulnerable person. Later: hospital staff, nursing homes, rescuers.
- What it sells, at what price: the TODO device at $200–$300. A subscription may be added later.
- Where and how: launch in the United States, sold online. Sales channel not yet decided. Device plus a companion app that connects wearer and caregiver.
- Budget and constraints: $10,000 starting budget; founder may need to raise more. No prototype yet. Unit cost unknown: estimate from comparable devices and mark it as an estimate.

### Features
- One-way camera: video goes from wearer to the caregiver's app.
- Two-way voice conversation between wearer and caregiver.
- SOS button.
- GPS location.
- Privacy mode: an adult wearer who does not need supervision can choose to share video only when they want to.
- Several ways to attach it to the body.
- "And more": further features not yet specified.

## What the board said

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass. Average score: 4.7 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 5)

### Risks more than one member raised
1. **No focus (all three).** Special-needs children, dementia, "any loved one", hospitals and rescuers are different products. Pick one wearer, one caregiver, one country.
2. **The wearer may not keep it on (all three).** People with dementia or an intellectual disability may refuse it, remove it, or fail to press SOS. If the device ends up in a drawer, nothing else matters.
3. **Connectivity and battery decide everything (all three).** Live video and voice need all-day battery and mobile data. Who pays for the data, and what it costs per month, is unknown, and that sets the subscription.
4. **No proof of a gap (Offers, Monopoly).** It is commodity parts (camera, GPS, SOS, app). Without a reason to choose TODO, it competes on price against trackers, kids' watches and SOS pendants.
5. **No channel (Offers, Monopoly).** Stressed caregivers are expensive to reach through ads, and nobody knows what each customer costs to acquire.

### Where the members disagree
- **Monopoly** is most worried about copycats and wants a moat beyond the hardware (partnerships, certifications, a trust brand).
- **Product** is most worried about the experience breaking at the wrong moment and wants the end-to-end flow written before anything is built.
- **Offers** is most worried about price competition and wants a guarantee and a stack that make TODO the obvious choice.

### The strongest version the board can see
Not a camera for everyone. A **"see, hear and find" safety device for one group, families of children with special needs who wander**, sold through the schools, therapists and parent groups those families already trust. The wearer does nothing except wear it: SOS is optional, and the caregiver gets a live view and voice the moment something is wrong. It comes with a clear monthly plan that covers the mobile data and a guarantee that it works when it matters. Dementia care is the second market, and nursing homes and hospitals come later, once certifications and a trusted brand exist. This is narrower than the pitch, which aims at every vulnerable person at once.

### 4. What customers complain about (AngelSense, the closest direct competitor)
1. **Battery: daily charging, about 16–24h** (strong: [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3268564/wearables/angelsense-gps-review.html) ~18h, "be diligent about charging … every night"; [G2](https://www.g2.com/products/angelsense/reviews) "typically lasts only a day"; [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/angelsense.com?page=2) "needs charging daily"; [Choosing Therapy](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/?p=105185) 16–24h).
2. **Limited voice minutes and contract lock-in** (thin: [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/angelsense.com?page=4) "only comes with 60 minutes of voice time"; 12-month contract with remaining payments due).
3. **Location errors and charging glitches** (thin: [Trustpilot](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/angelsense.com?page=7) "places the child in the wrong location", reboot through the app to fix charging).
4. **Lock-on strap: both loved and hated** (thin: praised for keeping a child with high-level autism safe, criticised because it can't be switched off; [Trustpilot](https://nl-be.trustpilot.com/review/angelsense.com)).
5. **Listen-in raises privacy and legal concerns** ([PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3268564/wearables/angelsense-gps-review.html)).
6. Kids' watches (Bark, Gabb) "struggled to make it through a full school day" in one test ([source](https://digikidz-com.pages.dev/best-kids-smartwatches)), thin.

Overall sentiment for AngelSense is high (4.5 on Trustpilot from 321 reviews), so the incumbent is liked, not hated.

### 5. The gap
**No product found gives a caregiver a live view from the wearer's body plus voice, SOS and GPS in a device built for vulnerable people.** Special-needs trackers stop at audio listen-in. Kids' watches do video *calls*, which need the wearer to answer and hold up their wrist, and that fails for the very people TODO is for. Body cams have the hardware but are built and sold for security. A search for dementia wearable cameras turned up only research prototypes ([Duquesne study](https://dsc.duq.edu/faculty/962)).

The catch is that **battery life is already the #1 complaint for audio-only devices**. Live video uses far more power and data, so TODO's gap only holds if it solves battery better than AngelSense, not worse. The privacy concerns around listen-in will be stronger for video, which makes TODO's privacy mode a selling point.

### 6. The threat
- **AngelSense** is the fastest copier. It already owns the buyer, the channel (special-needs parents) and a monthly plan. Adding a camera to its next device would erase TODO's gap.
- **TickTalk / Bark** could add a caregiver-initiated "auto-answer video" mode in software.
- **OEM body-cam factories** already sell 4G live-stream + GPS + 2-way audio hardware for ~$120–$130 wholesale, so a copycat brand could launch quickly. This is also good news, because TODO's white-label route looks feasible.

## Pricing

### 1. The price: **$249 device + $24.99/month (or $19.99/month paid yearly)**

- **Buyers:** the acceptable range is $91–$280 (`pricing-curve.md`). The median buyer starts to find it expensive at $249 and too expensive at $320. $249 is inside the range but at the top, so it needs a reason to believe (trial, proof).
- **Competitors:** devices run ~$100–$230. Special-needs and senior plans run $30–$65/month (AngelSense $44.99–$64.99; Medical Guardian $39.95–$46.95; Bay Alarm $29.95–$39.95). Kids' watches run $10–$18/month. At $24.99 TODO costs **less per month than every special-needs competitor** while being the only one with live video.
- **Margin (CFO tool, estimated costs):**

| device price | contribution per device | break-even | year 1 operating profit (device only) |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $199 | -$35.46 (loses money) | never | -$13,674 |
| **$249** | **$14.54 (6%)** | **2/day** | **-$3,699** |
| $299 | $64.54 (22%) | 1/day | +$6,276 |

  **The device alone barely breaks even at $249, because the estimated $60 of ads per sale eats the margin. The monthly plan is where the money is.** At ~$10/month of data and cloud cost per user (estimate) and 5% of subscribers cancelling each month, year 1 subscription contribution is about **$8,000 at $19.99** or **$12,000 at $24.99** on the same ramp. Together that turns year 1 positive. `/founder-cfo` models it properly.
- **Never go below $199 on the device.** At that price each sale loses money before fixed costs.

### 3. The stack
- **Core:** "See, hear and find your loved one the moment something is wrong." Device + app. GPS always on, two-way voice, one-press SOS, and live video **only during an SOS or a visible check-in**. Month-to-month from $24.99, no contract.
- **Bonus 1: Wear-It-Their-Way Kit.** Clip, lanyard, armband and a locking harness for wearers who pull things off.

### 6. Licences, permits, compliance (confirm each; not legal advice)
- **Business registration:** an LLC in your state (state Secretary of State website) + an [EIN from the IRS](https://www.irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online) (free).
- **Sales tax:** register in your state and any state where you pass economic nexus thresholds; Shopify can calculate it.
- **FCC:** cellular devices sold in the US need FCC equipment authorization ([47 CFR 2.1204](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/47/2.1204)). **Only buy a device that already has a valid FCC ID**; a white-label rebrand can often use the factory's grant, but confirm with the factory and the FCC rules.
- **Carrier certification:** check with your IoT SIM provider that the device module is approved on the US networks it will use.
- **COPPA (children's privacy):** the FTC treats children's **location, photos, video and audio** as personal information, and connected devices and apps are covered ([summary](https://adexchanger.com/data-exchanges/ftc-updates-coppa-with-new-restrictions-on-location-data-photos)). You need a clear privacy notice, **verifiable parental consent**, data security and deletion rules, and you're responsible for your vendors' SDKs. The FTC has warned kids' GPS-watch makers before. **Budget for a privacy lawyer review before launch.**
- **Recording consent laws:** about 11–13 states require all-party consent for audio recording (commonly listed: CA, DE, FL, IL, MD, MA, MT, NV, NH, PA, WA, with others in some lists) ([overview](https://www.getnexar.com/blog/dash-cam-privacy-laws-by-state)). Two-way calls the wearer joins are different from silent listen-in, but have a lawyer confirm. **Don't build a silent "listen-in" feature** without legal advice.
- **Health/medical claims:** don't market it as a medical device or claim it prevents harm, to stay clear of FDA medical-device rules and FTC advertising rules.
- **Insurance:** product liability + general liability + cyber/data breach. Get quotes from a small-business broker; $150/month in `numbers.json` is an estimate.
- **Battery shipping:** lithium batteries shipped inside or packed with equipment have carrier rules (USPS/UPS); check before shipping spares.
