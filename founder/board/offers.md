# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The pain is real and emotional.** A family that cannot see or reach a vulnerable person who has wandered off, fallen or panicked feels serious fear. Fear about a loved one is the kind of pain people pay to make go away.
- **The dream outcome is easy to state:** "see them, hear them, find them, the moment something is wrong." Live video plus two-way voice is a step beyond a location dot. The caregiver sees what is happening, not just where.
- **The buyer is not the user.** The caregiver pays and the wearer wears it, and caregivers usually spend more freely to protect someone else than to buy for themselves.
- **There is room to build a stack.** SOS, GPS, privacy mode and several ways to wear it are the start of a bundle that solves several problems, not one feature.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **The market is unpicked.** The brief puts children with special needs, people with dementia, "any loved one" and later hospitals and rescuers into one target. Those groups differ in pain, budget, where you reach them and who decides. With no launch country, no channel and no market size, none of the four market tests (massive pain, money to spend, easy to reach, growing) can be scored yet. A weak or blurred market is the most common reason this lens passes.
2. **Likelihood and effort are unproven.** A caregiver will ask: will it stay on, stay charged, stay connected, and work at the one moment it matters? A person with dementia or an intellectual disability may take it off, refuse it, or fail to press SOS. There is no proof, no pilot and no guarantee, so perceived likelihood is low. If the device needs daily charging and fitting, the effort cost lands on the people already carrying the most.
3. **It could end up competing on price.** Without a clear reason it is the obvious choice, a $200–300 camera sits next to cheaper trackers, watches and phones that buyers already own. Whether that is true is unknown (open question for /founder-competitors). On top of that, live video and voice need ongoing connectivity, and the subscription that would pay for it is undefined. The margin is unknown (open question for /founder-cfo).

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which one group first: special-needs children or dementia, and in which country? Who exactly signs the cheque?
- What happens in the first ten minutes of an emergency, step by step, and how long does the caregiver take to get a useful result?
- Why would a worried family believe it works? What proof can you show before launch: a pilot, a case story, an endorsement from a care professional?
- What does the wearer have to do every day (charge, attach, accept it), and what happens when they refuse?
- What is the guarantee? Could you double the price and still be the obvious choice?
- What is in the subscription, what does connectivity cost per unit per month, and can you deliver it profitably? (/founder-cfo)
- Would the caregivers you are aiming at actually buy at $200–300, and what makes them say no? (/founder-consumer)
- What do families use today instead, at what price, and what do they complain about? (/founder-competitors)

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Pick one beachhead segment and one country**, then show from /founder-consumer that it scores well on pain, money and reach, and /founder-competitors confirms a gap that is not just "cheaper".
2. **Build the stack for that segment.** List every problem that segment hits (wandering, refusal to wear, charging, false alarms, caregiver panic, privacy consent) and add a cheap fix for each. Add a guarantee the CFO can afford, such as "works when it matters or your money back", and give it a clear name.
3. **Raise likelihood with proof:** a small paid pilot with real families before scaling, with response times and outcomes recorded.
4. **Show positive unit economics including connectivity**, with a defined subscription, via /founder-cfo before any money goes into manufacturing.
