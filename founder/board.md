# Board summary: TODO

**Vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass. Average score: 4.7 / 10** (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 5)

## Risks more than one member raised
1. **No focus (all three).** Special-needs children, dementia, "any loved one", hospitals and rescuers are different products. Pick one wearer, one caregiver, one country.
2. **The wearer may not keep it on (all three).** People with dementia or an intellectual disability may refuse it, remove it, or fail to press SOS. If the device ends up in a drawer, nothing else matters.
3. **Connectivity and battery decide everything (all three).** Live video and voice need all-day battery and mobile data. Who pays for the data, and what it costs per month, is unknown, and that sets the subscription.
4. **No proof of a gap (Offers, Monopoly).** It is commodity parts (camera, GPS, SOS, app). Without a reason to choose TODO, it competes on price against trackers, kids' watches and SOS pendants.
5. **No channel (Offers, Monopoly).** Stressed caregivers are expensive to reach through ads, and nobody knows what each customer costs to acquire.

## Where the members disagree
- **Monopoly** is most worried about copycats and wants a moat beyond the hardware (partnerships, certifications, a trust brand).
- **Product** is most worried about the experience breaking at the wrong moment and wants the end-to-end flow written before anything is built.
- **Offers** is most worried about price competition and wants a guarantee and a stack that make TODO the obvious choice.

## Conditions checklist
- [ ] Pick one beachhead: one wearer group (e.g. children with special needs, or dementia), one caregiver, one country. Hospitals, nursing homes and rescuers move to the roadmap, and "and more" comes off the list. *(founder decision)*
- [ ] Show a real gap against existing products that isn't just "cheaper". *(/founder-competitors)*
- [ ] Caregivers in the beachhead score high on pain and will pay $200–300. *(/founder-consumer, /founder-pricing)*
- [ ] Decide how it connects (cellular, phone or Wi-Fi) and define the subscription from the data cost. *(/founder-cfo, /founder-offer)*
- [ ] Positive unit economics including connectivity, before money goes into manufacturing. *(/founder-cfo)*
- [ ] Build the offer stack: a fix for each problem (wandering, refusal to wear, charging, false alarms, consent), an affordable guarantee and a clear name. *(/founder-offer)*
- [ ] A named channel to the first 100 customers (special-needs schools, therapists, support groups, care organisations) with an estimated cost per customer. *(/founder-marketing)*
- [ ] At least one moat beyond the hardware: care partnerships, certifications for later B2B, or a trust brand. *(/founder-competitors, /founder-brand)*
- [ ] Write the end-to-end experience: unboxing, pairing, a normal day, charging, an SOS, a lost signal, each with an owner and a fallback. *(/founder-ops)*
- [ ] A small paid real-world pilot proving wearers keep it on daily for several weeks, with response times and outcomes recorded. *(/founder-launch)*

## The strongest version the board can see
Not a camera for everyone. A **"see, hear and find" safety device for one group, families of children with special needs who wander**, sold through the schools, therapists and parent groups those families already trust. The wearer does nothing except wear it: SOS is optional, and the caregiver gets a live view and voice the moment something is wrong. It comes with a clear monthly plan that covers the mobile data and a guarantee that it works when it matters. Dementia care is the second market, and nursing homes and hospitals come later, once certifications and a trusted brand exist. This is narrower than the pitch, which aims at every vulnerable person at once.
